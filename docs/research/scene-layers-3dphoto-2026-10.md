# Scene layers: how single-image 3D photo systems build layers without tearing — 2026-10-24

Research date: 2026-10-24. Method: full paper text read via ar5iv / arXiv HTML for One Shot 3D Photography (2008.12298), 3D Photo Inpainting (2004.04727), 3D Ken Burns (1909.05483), SLIDE (2109.01068), Single-view MPI (2004.11364), AdaMPI (2205.11733), MuLAn (2404.02790), LayerPano3D (2408.13252), Qwen-Image-Layered (2512.15603) and its model card. Abstracts only for S-MPI, SHARP, RevealLayer, Scene4U, InstaOrder, Layered Depth Refinement, LDI (1998), Silberman 2012. Anything resting on a snippet or vendor page is **[unverified]**. Nothing was downloaded or run. The recommendations are **reasoning**, not measurements.

Zumi context: panels become 2-8 flat RGBA cut-outs (optional per-layer depth map in .zumi). Characters and lettering are solved. The problem is the rest of the scene. Failed so far: depth-histogram bands (cut floors), SAM point-grid (over-segmentation), LLM-named elements + SAM 3.1 text prompts (detached pieces, missing names).

## Executive summary

1. **No 3D photo system cuts the image into layers by depth value. They cut only where neighbouring pixels jump in disparity.** One Shot 3D Photography marks a discontinuity when a pixel's neighbour differs by more than τ = 0.05 in normalised disparity ([Kopf et al. 2020, §4.2.1](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2008.12298)). A floor changes disparity by a tiny amount per pixel, so it never crosses τ and stays one connected surface. A depth-histogram band has no notion of neighbours, which is why it slices floors.
2. **Their "layers" are local, not global.** Kopf and Shih both use a Layered Depth Image: one layer almost everywhere, extra layers only behind discontinuities, no fixed count ([Shih et al. 2020](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2004.04727); [Shade et al. 1998](https://grail.cs.washington.edu/projects/ldi/)). Zumi needs closed, global cut-outs, so we must add a grouping step they do not have.
3. **Keeping objects whole is done by an explicit rule, never by depth alone.** 3D Ken Burns gives each person/car/animal mask *one* depth, "the smallest depth value from the bottom of the salient object", i.e. a frontal plane standing on the ground ([Niklaus et al. 2019, §3.1.2](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1909.05483)). Kopf merges small components into "foreground or background, whichever has a larger contact surface".
4. **Detached fragments are removed by size thresholds, everywhere.** Kopf: components < 20 px merged, discontinuity curves < 20 px dropped. Shih: depth edges < 10 px removed (tuned by cross-validation on LPIPS). MuLAn: instances whose largest connected component is < 20 px or 0.1 % of the image dropped ([Tudosiu et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.02790v1)).
5. **Tearing is avoided by growing the background behind every foreground edge before inpainting**, not by better cuts. Kopf grows the hidden layer 50 one-pixel iterations behind each discontinuity; Shih grows a synthesis region 40 iterations and a context region 100. SLIDE replaces the hard cut by a soft alpha from the disparity gradient ([Jampani et al. 2021](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2109.01068)).
6. **The closest published recipe to what Zumi needs is LayerPano3D**: panoptic segmentation (OneFormer, ADE20K) → one depth per asset (75th percentile of the mask) → cluster assets into N = 3 layers; sky/floor/ceiling are "background categories" kept out of the clustering ([Yang et al. 2024/SIGGRAPH 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.13252v2)). MuLAn is the same shape with a detector + SAM and a flat background layer, and only 36 % of its LAION decompositions were judged good.
7. **Layer counts in the literature are small for object layers**: SLIDE 2, LayerPano3D 3, MuLAn ≈ 3.3 per image on average. Fronto-parallel MPIs need 32 planes (Tucker & Snavely) and still distort slanted surfaces.
8. **Fixed depth planes split objects.** AdaMPI shows a fixed-spacing MPI cutting a Buddha's face across two planes; adapting plane depths to the scene fixes it ([Han et al. 2022](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2205.11733)). S-MPI exists because fronto-parallel layers fail "for surfaces imaged at oblique angles" ([Zhang et al. CVPR 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.05937)).
9. **Everything that matters here is cheap.** Kopf's full pipeline (depth, filter, components, hidden geometry, inpainting, meshing) ran in 1.1 s on an iPhone 11 Pro. The grouping logic is graph work on CPU on top of Depth Anything 3 and the SAM masks Zumi already has.

Related work that is not 3D photography but answers Zumi's sub-questions:

- **Ordering: occlusion order ≠ depth order.** InstaOrder annotates both on 101 K scenes and finds them "complementary... neither alone can fully depict the geometric relationship" ([Lee & Park, CVPR 2022](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2022/html/Lee_Instance-Wise_Occlusion_and_Depth_Orders_in_Natural_Scenes_CVPR_2022_paper.html)). MuLAn sorts by depth, then corrects so occluded instances come before their occluders, and orders mutual occluders by max depth.
- **"Things resting on a table" is called support inference.** Silberman et al. parse indoor scenes into floor, walls, supporting surfaces and objects, and infer which region supports which with an integer program ([ECCV 2012](https://cs.nyu.edu/~fergus/datasets/indoor_seg_support.pdf)). Needs RGB-D; the idea (support = contact at the object's bottom) transfers.
- **Depth refinement inside a mask.** Layered Depth Refinement splits depth into the mask layer and its inverse and inpaints each, fixing blurred boundaries and holes ([Kim et al., CVPR 2022](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2022/papers/Kim_Layered_Depth_Refinement_With_Mask_Guidance_CVPR_2022_paper.pdf)). Useful if DA3 edges bleed across a cut-out.
- **Comics depth.** Bhattacharjee et al. found text causes depth artefacts in comic panels and add a text/image split ([WACV 2022](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2022/html/Bhattacharjee_Estimating_Image_Depth_in_the_Comics_Domain_WACV_2022_paper.html)). Zumi already masks lettering, so mask it out of depth statistics too.
- **Newer generative decomposers** (RevealLayer, May 2026, natural images, diffusion, no code link found ([arXiv 2605.11818](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11818)); Scene4U, open-vocab segmentation + LLM on panoramas ([arXiv 2504.00387](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.00387))) share MuLAn's shape. None publishes a floor-handling rule in its abstract **[unverified beyond abstract]**.

## 2. Answers to the key questions

**How do they choose boundaries?** Local disparity jumps (Kopf, Shih), soft alpha from the disparity gradient (SLIDE), or semantic segments (Ken Burns, LayerPano3D, MuLAn). The two families are complementary: jumps find *where* to cut, segments say *what is one thing*. No system uses a global depth histogram.

**How do they keep an object and what touches it together?** Contact. Kopf's merge rule picks the side with the larger contact surface; Ken Burns pins an object to the depth where its bottom touches the ground. At a contact line depth is continuous, so the jump detector never fires there (mechanism: a cup on a table shares disparity with the tabletop along its base).

**How many layers?** 2-3 object layers plus a background in every segment-based system. Only plane-sweep MPIs use 32+.

**Floors without seams?** Either never cut them (Kopf/Shih mesh is continuous), keep them out of the object layers (LayerPano3D, MuLAn background), or give the layer a depth map (LDI "sprites with depth"). Fronto-parallel slicing is the documented failure (S-MPI, AdaMPI).

**What is cheap enough?** All the grouping and cleanup rules above are image-processing on a 700 px depth map. The expensive parts in the literature are inpainting (Flux-Fill, SD 1.5) and learned decomposers (Qwen-Image-Layered 20 B).

## 3. Recommendations for Zumi (reasoning; test on 20 panels before trusting)

1. **Work in normalised disparity, not linear depth.** DA3 gives linear depth; the published thresholds (τ = 0.05) are in disparity = 1/depth rescaled to [0, 1]. A 1 m step is a large jump up close and a negligible one at 30 m, which matches what parallax will show.
2. **Use the SAM point-grid over-segmentation as superpixels, then merge.** Build a region adjacency graph. For each shared boundary, measure the fraction of boundary pixels whose disparity jump exceeds τ (after Kopf's 5×5 edge-aware median). Merge across mostly continuous boundaries (start at < 50 % jumping; tune). Mechanism: floor pieces merge (no jumps); a cup merges with its table (contact, small jump); a table separates from the floor behind it (jumps along the tabletop edge, continuity only at leg tips). This turns the over-segmentation that failed into the input it is good at being.
3. **Let the room shell be one layer with its depth map.** Floor + walls + ceiling + sky will often chain-merge through continuous corners. Do not fight it: that is LayerPano3D's and MuLAn's background layer. Write it as the back layer with the optional per-layer depth map (LDI sprites with depth) so a floor recedes smoothly instead of standing upright. Back layer always opaque, as in Tucker & Snavely.
4. **One depth per object cut-out: contact depth or 75th percentile, not the mean.** Ken Burns' bottom-of-object rule for things on the ground; LayerPano3D's 75th percentile as the fallback for things hanging or floating. The mean drags a long object toward its far end.
5. **Cluster merged regions into layers by depth, never split a region.** 1-D k-means (or largest-gap split) on per-region depth, K = 2-4 object layers plus the shell, characters and lettering inserted by their own depth. A region is atomic once merged.
6. **Order by occlusion first, depth second.** At each boundary with a jump, the nearer side occludes. Topologically sort that graph; break cycles by MuLAn's rule (max depth). Use cluster depth only for parallax amount.
7. **Kill fragments by size and contact.** Any component < 20 px or < 0.1 % of the panel joins the neighbour with the longest shared boundary (Kopf, MuLAn).
8. **Prevent tearing by filling behind, not by better masks.** Grow each layer's hidden region outward a fixed margin behind its occluders (Kopf: 50 one-pixel wavefront iterations; the LDI resolution that 50 refers to was not checked, so tune the margin at 700 px) and inpaint it before export. Feather alpha 1-2 px or use SLIDE's exp(−β‖∇D‖²) at cut-out edges.
9. **Optional semantic hint, Meta-licensed:** a panoptic model with ADE20K "stuff" classes (floor, wall, sky, table) can seed the merge graph the way LayerPano3D uses OneFormer. Mask2Former is FAIR's ([Cheng et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.01527)); its behaviour on webtoon art is untested.

## 4. What not to do

- **Global depth bands or fixed depth planes as the partition.** They split continuous surfaces and faces (AdaMPI's Buddha, S-MPI's oblique surfaces). Depth decides a layer's *offset*, not its *membership*.
- **A list of names as the partition** (LLM names → text-prompted masks). MuLAn, a careful version of exactly this (open-vocabulary detector + SAM + depth), passed manual review on 36 % of LAION images (52 % counting minor defects); its main failures were segmentation, detection and "irrelevant decompositions". Whatever no name covers is left behind.
- **Mean depth per mask** for objects with large depth extent (floors, long tables).
- **Hard cuts with no hidden-region fill.** Every 3D photo paper spends its effort here, because a cut without fill shows a hole the moment the head moves.
- **Generative layer decomposers as the main path on a 3060.** Qwen-Image-Layered is 20 B and benchmarked on graphic designs; LayerPano3D/MuLAn-style diffusion inpainting per layer is the slow part of those pipelines. Revisit only for the background fill step, and only if a cheaper inpainter fails.
