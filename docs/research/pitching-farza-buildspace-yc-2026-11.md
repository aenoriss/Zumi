# Pitching like Farza, buildspace and Y Combinator — 2026-11-17

Research date: 2026-11-17. Public sources only; no paid calls. Purpose: shape Zumi's competition submission (a 2-3 minute demo video plus a written entry) using what Farza Majeed, buildspace and Y Combinator actually told builders.

Conventions: **[primary]** read in the source that owns the claim (the person's or organisation's own page, essay or post). **[secondary]** a third party's summary or account; treat wording as approximate. **[no primary found]** looked for and not found. Quotes are exact and short.

## The answer in five lines

1. **Say what it is in the first sentence, in plain words.** All three agree. PG: "Say what you're doing as soon as possible, preferably in the first sentence" ([How to Present to Investors](http://www.paulgraham.com/investors.html)) **[primary]**. buildspace's demo-day spec opens with a ~10 s one-liner ([S5 week 6](https://buildspace.so/s5/week/6)) **[primary]**.
2. **Show the thing working, then show progress.** buildspace's 90 s format is intro 10 s, demo 30-45 s, progress 30-45 s, next steps 10 s, and the progress section says "show, don't tell" ([S5 week 6](https://buildspace.so/s5/week/6)) **[primary]**.
3. **Three or four memorable points, no more.** YC's Demo Day guide builds the talk on 3-4 "vertebrae" and ends by restating them ([Ralston 2016](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/guide-to-demo-day-pitches/)) **[primary]**; Hale limits a deck to 5-7 ideas because the audience remembers one or two ([Hale 2015](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/how-to-design-a-better-pitch-deck)) **[primary]**.
4. **The first three seconds decide whether anyone keeps watching.** Farza's "did my eyes stop" test ([YC Startup School, Oct 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v12aSIZIXUs), chapter 08:34) — the chapter title is confirmed; its content is known only from summaries **[secondary]**.
5. **Audio quality matters more than picture quality.** buildspace: "good audio" first in its production tips ([S5 week 6](https://buildspace.so/s5/week/6)) **[primary]**; Farza in the YC talk, chapter 40:49 "audio matters most" **[secondary]**.

Chapters that bear on Zumi: "Demos versus launches" (02:56), "The 'did my eyes stop' test" (08:34), "Scripting videos that sound unscripted" (09:58), "Being willing to look cringe" (28:11), "Beats, not cuts" (37:07), "Gear, and why audio matters most" (40:49).

### What summaries say he said **[secondary]**

From [Summify](https://summify.io/discover/this-is-how-you-should-actually-make-launch-videos-v12aSI) and [Sean Weldon](https://www.sean-weldon.com/blog/2026-10-25-this-is-how-you-should-actually-make-launch-videos), both summaries of the same talk:

- **Demo, don't launch.** Ship many small "demos" rather than one polished launch video. A phone video of Clicky reportedly reached about 3 million views and 7,000 downloads, against a ~20,000 USD produced video that did worse.
- **The scroll test.** In a feed, the first three seconds filter everything. His check is whether his own eyes stop.
- **Formula:** roughly "it's me, here's what's going on, here's what I'm doing" — who you are, the context and the stakes in the first 10-20 % of the video.
- **Script fully, then rehearse until it sounds unscripted.**
- **Audio over visuals.** One summary quotes "If your audio is bad, I'm bouncing" without naming the speaker.
- **Write the hook before you build the feature.**

### In his own words, from his Substack **[primary]**

In [Aug 2025](https://farza.substack.com/p/making-tiktoks-with-the-lads-and) he wrote that his early Reels failed because he was "trying way to hard to tell a story that wasn't there yet", and that "TikTok has an extremely different culture" — each platform needs its own cut. Lesson for Zumi: don't invent a founder narrative; the transformation is the story.

## 2. buildspace Nights & Weekends

buildspace is closed; its home page now reads as a farewell ([buildspace.so](https://buildspace.so/)) **[primary]**. Its YC page: N&W was "a six-week program" where people start an idea on their own time and should end with first fans, revenue or downloads; 30,000+ joined in 2023 ([YC company page](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/buildspace)) **[primary]**.

### What builders were asked for (Season 5, July 2024) **[primary]**

All from the surviving [S5 week 6 page](https://buildspace.so/s5/week/6) and [S5 week 1 page](https://buildspace.so/s5/week/1):

**The demo-day video**, "a max of 90s", "16:9, horizontal":

| Part | Length | What goes in |
|---|---|---|
| Intro | ~10 s | Your one-liner; you fill the frame, centred, plain background |
| Demo | 30-45 s | How it works, for someone seeing it the first time; screen must be readable |
| Progress | 30-45 s | Numbers, or milestones if no numbers; what failed and what worked; "show, don't tell" |
| Next steps | ~10 s | Where it goes, how to follow it |

- Production: good audio, good lighting (not "in a dungeon"), a steady camera angle.
- A custom thumbnail (it is what people see on the demo page) and a title in the style "how I went from X to Z in 6 weeks".
- Social post: the first line carries the hook; don't be "overly clever".
- **Live demo day:** rooms of at most 7 on Discord; each person shares a screen for a ~2-minute live demo, then gets feedback. "just embrace the cringe".
- **Grants:** an optional 45-90 s *vertical* video was "the #1 thing" looked at when choosing grant recipients.
- **Week 1:** an idea slide posted publicly, to get the idea "out of your head and into the world", plus honest feedback to three others ("don't just say: 'great idea!'").

## 3. Y Combinator

### The one sentence

- **Seibel's email test:** write two sentences, send them to a smart friend; if they ask a clarifying question, rewrite. "Your elevator pitch should be like baby food." Cut problem setup from "what do you do", and cut "platform" and jargon ([How to Pitch Your Company, 2016](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/how-to-pitch-your-company/)) **[primary]**.
- **PG on the application:** "The best answers are the most matter of fact"; "It's a mistake to use marketing-speak". A good description lets the reader almost rebuild the project; describing it as a variant of something familiar works; start narrow ([How to Apply](https://www.ycombinator.com/howtoapply)) **[primary]**.
- **PG on talks:** start with a narrow, concrete description and widen it; "Your primary goal is not to describe everything your system might one day become" ([How to Present to Investors](http://www.paulgraham.com/investors.html)) **[primary]**.
- **Ralston's example:** "We deliver groceries to customers in their homes" beats an "AI-based resolver of grocery needs" ([Demo Day guide](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/guide-to-demo-day-pitches/)) **[primary]**.
- The application's "Describe what your company does in 50 characters or less" is attested by alumni guides and example collections ([Shizune](https://shizune.co/yc-application-examples/describe-what-your-company-does-in-50-characters-or-less)) **[secondary]**; the form itself is behind login.

### The application's questions **[primary, partial]**

PG's essay names "What is your company going to make?", something impressive each founder built, the non-computer system you hacked, other ideas you had, plus an optional video and demo: "Statistically we're much more likely to interview people who submit a video" ([How to Apply](https://www.ycombinator.com/howtoapply)). The current form's full list is **[no primary found]**.

### Pitch structure

**Seibel's seven questions**, in order: what you do, market size (bottom-up), progress, unique insight, business model (one), team, the ask. "I'm much more interested in progress than genius ideas" ([2016](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/how-to-pitch-your-company/)) **[primary]**.

**Ralston's Demo Day order:** what you do and why it matters (a striking number may open, "Don't bury the lead"), brief problem, product and customer, the 3-4 vertebrae (what, for whom, why not done yet, why hard, why now), opportunity, traction, team, model, then "Do not conclude with a whimper but rather with a bang." He tells founders to avoid videos on stage because investors want to see them live ([2016](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/guide-to-demo-day-pitches/)) **[primary]**.

**Hale's slides:** legible, simple, obvious. One idea per slide; a stranger must state the slide's idea at a glance, "If they don't immediately say your idea, you lose." Demo Day was 2 min 30 s in a 500-seat room with ~100 companies. "Screenshots are almost always illegible, complex, and non-obvious"; write the conclusion on a graph instead of making people read it ([2015](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/how-to-design-a-better-pitch-deck)) **[primary]**. Hale's 2019 talk "How to Pitch Your Startup" (Startup School) covers clarity, "X for Y" and concision by its chapter titles; its content is known only from summaries ([Summify](https://summify.io/discover/kevin-hale-how-to-pitch-your-startup-17XZGU)) **[secondary]**.

**PG on demos and delivery:** "A demo explains what you've made more effectively than any verbal description"; have one person talk while another drives; slides under 20 words; use a few specific numbers about your own company, not market-size figures; tell user stories; confidence by showing, "And I mean show, not tell" ([How to Present to Investors](http://www.paulgraham.com/investors.html)) **[primary]**. In [How to Convince Investors](https://www.paulgraham.com/convince.html) (2013): "Make the truth good, then just tell it" **[primary]**.

## Synthesis

**Where they agree**
- What it is, first, in plain words. No setup, no buzzwords.
- Demonstration beats description (PG's demo line; buildspace's "show, don't tell").
- Progress over ambition: Seibel's "progress than genius ideas"; buildspace's progress section; PG on not describing what it "might one day become".
- Few points, repeated: Ralston's vertebrae, Hale's 5-7 ideas, buildspace's 4-part 90 s.
- Honesty: Ralston bans exaggeration; PG's "make the truth good"; buildspace asks for what failed.

**Where they differ**
- **Video vs live.** YC distrusts video and screencasts on stage (Ralston, Hale); buildspace and Farza live by video. Zumi's medium is fixed (a video), so take YC's reason, not its rule: footage of a screen must be legible at a glance.
- **Polish.** Farza and buildspace favour raw, frequent, slightly cringe; YC favours a rehearsed, single-message talk. Both want rehearsal: Farza scripts then rehearses until it sounds unscripted **[secondary]**.
- **The person.** buildspace puts the builder's face in the first 10 s; YC centres the company, with the team later. For a competition video, a few seconds of the maker is cheap and adds trust.
- **Audience.** YC: investors deciding whom to meet. buildspace: a feed of strangers. A competition jury sits between: a captive audience, but judging dozens of entries, like Hale's 100-company room.

## Checklist for Zumi

### (a) The 2-3 minute demo video

**First 10 seconds** (the scroll test and the one-liner):
- [ ] Frame 1 is the pencil page, real paper, in a hand. By second 5-8 it is playing in colour through a portal in a real wall, with a voice line audible. The transformation *is* the hook; don't spend it on a logo or a title card.
- [ ] One spoken or captioned sentence, by second 10: "Zumi turns a hand-drawn pencil comic into an episode that plays in your room." Test it on a friend (Seibel's email test).
- [ ] Optionally 2-3 s of the maker on camera (buildspace's intro), never more.

**Demo, ~60-80 s** (buildspace's 30-45 s, stretched for a 2-3 min budget):
- [ ] One continuous episode moment shot through the headset (mixed-reality capture): portal opens, panel plays, balloon voiced, music under it. Long takes show it's real; "beats, not cuts" **[secondary]**.
- [ ] Before/after pairs at the steps a viewer can see: pencil → colour; flat panel → depth layers moving with the head; silent balloon → voice. One step per shot, captioned in a few words (Hale: one idea per slide, conclusion written on it).
- [ ] Footage legible on a laptop at 50 %: no tiny UI, no full-screen pipeline logs. If the pipeline is shown, it is a 5-second diagram, not a screencast (Hale on screenshots).

**Proof, ~30-40 s** (buildspace's progress section; Seibel's progress; Ralston's traction):
- [ ] Measured numbers, said once and written on screen: pages processed, end-to-end time per page or episode, cost per episode, accuracy of speaker attribution and reading order on held-out pages (memory notes record 511/524 and 84/86 as of 2026-11-02 — confirm the definitions before quoting). PG: a few specific numbers about your own work.
- [ ] Runs on a stock Quest 3 (state the frame rate if measured), and a release build exists (commit befb179).
- [ ] If anyone outside the team has watched it in the headset, one line or a 3-second clip of their reaction. No reactions yet → skip; don't fake traction.
- [ ] One honest "what failed" beat if it teaches something (buildspace asks for it); one sentence, no more.

**Ending, ~10-15 s**:
- [ ] Restate the 3 vertebrae, e.g. *drawn by hand · comes alive by itself · plays in your room* (Ralston: end with a bang, restate).
- [ ] Final frame: the pencil page beside the living portal, the name, and where to see more.

**Production**:
- [ ] Audio first: a real mic for narration, music ducked under voice lines.
- [ ] Script it, rehearse until it sounds unscripted.
- [ ] Use the team's own comic only. Pepper&Carrot is internal R&D and its author bans AI derivations (memory: P&C licence); it must not appear on screen.
- [ ] A custom thumbnail: pencil half / colour half.

### (b) The written submission

- [ ] **First sentence is the one-liner**, matter of fact (PG). A ≤50-character version for title fields: "Pencil comics that play in your room, on Quest 3" (48 characters).
- [ ] **Second sentence: how, narrowly**: "An AI pipeline colours the pages, finds panels, balloons and speakers, splits each panel into depth layers, and adds voices, music and sound; the reader watches it through a portal in their wall." Then stop widening.
- [ ] **The insight** (Seibel's question 4): one sentence on what you know that others don't. Candidate: comics already contain the script, the cast and the shot list, so the episode can be derived, not authored. State it plainly, no "passion" rhetoric.
- [ ] **Progress, in numbers** (same figures as the video, with how they were measured, held-out pages only).
- [ ] **Why Quest 3 / mixed reality** in two sentences: the room as stage; head-tracked depth is what a flat screen can't give.
- [ ] **Team**: only relevant credentials (Seibel).
- [ ] **What's next**: one paragraph, concrete (e.g. an episode a reader's own drawing can produce end to end), not a vision of everything it might become (PG).
- [ ] Every claim true and checkable; link the video in the first paragraph.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Opening with the problem or the vision** ("Comics have been static for a century..."). Say what Zumi does first (Seibel, Ralston).
- **A logo sting or title card in the first 3 seconds.** It fails the scroll test **[secondary]** and buries the lead (Ralston).
- **Listing every pipeline stage.** Ten steps is ten ideas; the jury will remember two (Hale). Pick the three visible transformations.
- **Pipeline screencasts and code** as proof. Illegible at a glance (Hale); show outputs, not tooling.
- **Jargon**: "multimodal agentic pipeline", "platform", "AI-powered" in the first sentence (Seibel, Ralston, PG).
- **Inflated or undefined numbers** (accuracy on the tuning set, "hours saved"). Exaggeration is the one thing every source bans.
- **Bad audio** over a beautiful shot.
- **Ending with a fade** or a list of thanks instead of the three points and the final image (Ralston).
- **Forcing a founder story** that isn't there yet (Farza, Aug 2025) **[primary]**.
