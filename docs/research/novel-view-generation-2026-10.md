# Novel view generation for depth-mesh panels — 2026-10-25

Research date: 2026-10-25. Method: model cards, official repos and arXiv abstracts read via web fetch. Nothing was downloaded or run, so every quality judgement below is **reasoning, not measurement**. Claims resting on a vendor blog, aggregator or snippet are marked **[unverified]**.

Zumi context: each panel is a textured mesh from Depth Anything 3. A sideways head move (5-20°, or a lateral offset) exposes pixels the panel never drew (behind characters, objects) and stretches surfaces that were seen edge-on. We want a model that renders "the same panel from a slightly different camera" faithfully, so its output can fill holes, add side detail, or feed back into the texture/depth.

## Executive summary

1. **The model the user remembers is almost certainly Qwen-Image-Edit-2509 plus the community "Multiple-angles" LoRA by dx8152** (Chinese author, Chinese prompts such as 将镜头向左旋转45度, "rotate the camera 45° left"; Apache-2.0; ~70.6k downloads last month) ([model card](https://huggingface.co/dx8152/Qwen-Edit-2509-Multiple-angles)). Its successor is fal's LoRA for Qwen-Image-Edit-2511, which takes `<sks> front-right quarter view eye-level shot medium shot` style prompts ([model card](https://huggingface.co/fal/Qwen-Image-Edit-2511-Multiple-Angles-LoRA)).
2. **Two families exist, and they fail differently.** *Pure generation* (Qwen multi-angle, SEVA, Seedream) repaints the whole image from a pose token, so nothing guarantees pixel alignment with our mesh. *Geometry-conditioned* (ViewCrafter, Uni3C, Gen3C, Voyager, See3D, NVS-Solver) first renders a depth point cloud at the new pose, then lets the model fill what is missing. Zumi already has the depth, so the second family is the one that fits.
3. **For 5-20° the holes are thin strips.** Warp-and-inpaint keeps every visible pixel identical by construction. A full regeneration redraws line art and lettering at every angle. That is the core argument for the ranking below.
4. **Licences eliminate most of the strong video models.** Stable Virtual Camera: non-commercial, including outputs ([HF](https://huggingface.co/stabilityai/stable-virtual-camera)). TrajectoryCrafter: "refrain from using it for any commercial or production purposes" ([License_Model](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/TrajectoryCrafter/TrajectoryCrafter/main/License_Model)). Voyager: excludes EU, UK and South Korea ([LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/HunyuanWorld-Voyager/main/LICENSE)). Lyra 2.0: no production use ([HF](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/Lyra-2.0)). Apache/MIT survivors: Qwen-Image-Edit family, ViewCrafter, Uni3C, Wan2.1-Fun Camera, ReCamMaster.
5. **Nothing found reports results on comics, manga or line art** for any of these models. Training data is real scenes (video models) or Gaussian-splat renders of objects (fal LoRA: "3,000+ Gaussian Splatting renders", 96 poses). Style fidelity on webtoons is **untested**.
6. **No Meta model does single-image novel view synthesis for scenes.** The closest Meta work is SAM 3D Objects (masked object → textured 3D, released 2025-11-19, SAM License) ([HF](https://huggingface.co/facebook/sam-3d-objects)), plus Meta's 3D Photos warp-and-inpaint pipeline (covered in [scene-layers-3dphoto-2026-10.md](scene-layers-3dphoto-2026-10.md)).

## 2. How faithful is each family, by mechanism

- **Pose-token image editors (Qwen multi-angle).** The model sees the panel and a text label for the target angle, then denoises a whole new image. Identity and palette carry over through the image condition, but every pixel is regenerated, so ink lines, screentone and lettering are redrawn rather than moved. The fal LoRA was trained on 45° bins: a request for `horizontal_angle: 10` asks for an angle it never saw. That small angles interpolate cleanly is **[unverified]**. A third-party anime workflow advises "start with small rotations" and says near-180° gets soft ([floyo](https://floyo.ai/workflows/qwen-edit-2511-multi-angle-camera-fo-bzj935i0wdlm)) [unverified].
- **Warp-conditioned video models (ViewCrafter, Uni3C, GEN3C, Voyager).** Pixels visible in the source arrive at the target pose through the point-cloud render, so the model mostly copies them and generates only holes and stretched regions. Producing a whole sweep in one video also gives consistency *between* views, which independent single-image calls do not. The costs are a low output resolution (320×512 to 576×1024 against our full panels) and VAE re-encoding of the visible pixels too, which softens line art. Mitigation: keep the original pixels and take only the hole pixels from the model.
- **Pure NVS (SEVA, SV3D, Zero123++).** These have no depth input, so the new view need not agree with *our* DA3 geometry. SV3D and Zero123++ are object-centric (Objaverse), not scenes.

## 3. Ranked top 3 for Zumi

1. **Depth-warp + masked inpaint, with Qwen-Image-Edit-2511 as the inpainter** (Apache-2.0, 20B). Render the DA3 mesh at the most extreme pose the viewer can reach (for example ±20° yaw and the lateral limit). The disoccluded pixels form a mask. Inpaint only that mask with the full panel as context, then write the result into a **background layer behind each depth edge** (Kopf/Shih LDI style), not into each view. One fill serves every head position, so views cannot disagree. Runs locally on the 3060 if a 4-bit build fits (8-12 GB claimed, **[unverified]**), otherwise on fal at ~$0.03/MP or on Modal. Why first: visible pixels are untouched, output resolution is ~1 MP+, the licence is clean, and the fill is drawn by an image model that has seen illustration.
2. **ViewCrafter, with DUSt3R replaced by our DA3 point cloud** (Apache-2.0). Generate a 25-frame sweep across the head-motion range and harvest only hole pixels and grazing-angle surfaces from it. These are multi-view consistent by construction. The 576×1024 model needs 23.5 GB and ~120 s per panel on an A100, so it runs on Modal (A100 time at ~2 min/panel; check the ≤ 2 USD/day guard). Use it where option 1's single fill looks flat, for example deep scenes with several overlapping layers. **Uni3C** is the higher-quality Apache-2.0 alternative (Wan2.1-14B, same warp idea), but at ~20 min on an H20 per clip it is roughly 10× ViewCrafter's cost (reasoning from the two repos' timings).
3. **Qwen-Image-Edit-2511 + fal Multiple-Angles LoRA** (the model the user remembers). Use it for content the warp cannot invent: the unseen side of a character or prop at 30-45°. Feed the result to SAM 3D Objects or a back-projection step as a reference, not as texture directly. Its output cannot be fused pixel-for-pixel, because the new view does not agree with DA3 geometry.

Not recommended for shipping: SEVA (non-commercial), TrajectoryCrafter (research only), Voyager (excludes EU/UK/KR, 60 GB), Lyra 2.0 (no production), ReCamMaster and TrajectoryCrafter (video input only).

## 4. Combining generated views with the original

1. **Warp.** Unproject the panel with DA3 depth and intrinsics. Reproject to the target pose; z-buffer; mark holes (no source pixel) and *stretch* (triangles whose screen area grew by more than a factor k, for example 4×).
2. **Grow the mask** behind depth edges by a few pixels (Shih grows 40 px for synthesis, 100 px for context; see [scene-layers-3dphoto](scene-layers-3dphoto-2026-10.md)), so halos around characters are regenerated too.
3. **Generate.** Option 1: inpaint the masked warp. Option 2: ViewCrafter sweep conditioned on the warp renders.
4. **Composite.** Keep source pixels wherever they are visible. Take generated pixels only inside the mask, with a feathered seam (Poisson or alpha) to hide the boundary.
5. **Fuse into the mesh.** Back-project the generated hole pixels onto a hidden background layer (an extra mesh shell behind each foreground cut-out). If option 2 is used, give each texel the view that sees it most frontally, weighted by cos(view angle).
6. **Depth for the new content.** Run DA3 again on the composited view and align it to the known depth on the overlap (scale/shift fit), so filled regions get geometry. Voyager is the only model that emits depth with RGB, but its licence rules it out.
7. **Check.** Re-render at the original pose: it must reproduce the source panel exactly (residual ≈ 0 outside the hidden layer). This is a free, automatic regression test.

## 5. Risks

- **Hallucinated content.** Behind a character the model invents whatever is plausible: a second arm, a door, text. Keep masks tight, prompt with "background continuation, no new objects", and run a VLM spot check (see [reviewer-heldout-test-2026-10.md](reviewer-heldout-test-2026-10.md)).
- **Style drift.** All candidates were trained on photos, video or 3D renders. Flat colour, screentone and ink weight may come back as soft gradients. Untested; benchmark on ~10 panels before committing.
- **Inconsistency between views.** Independent per-angle calls (Qwen multi-angle) disagree with each other. Filling one hidden layer, or running one video sweep, avoids this.
- **Lettering and bubbles.** These must be excluded from the generation input, or masked, or the model will redraw or invent glyphs. Zumi already separates them (`bubbles.py`, `textcrops.py`).
- **Geometry mismatch.** Pose-token outputs do not follow DA3 depth, and fusing them makes ghosting. Only warp-conditioned outputs go into the texture.
- **Resolution.** Video NVS models top out around 576×1024. That is fine for thin hole strips, but too soft to replace visible pixels.
- **Licence drift.** Check the LoRA licence (Apache-2.0 today) and the fal terms at ship time. Qwen-Image-2.x weights are research-licensed per [generative-models-2026-10.md](generative-models-2026-10.md), so do not upgrade blindly.
