# Visual language of "lofi" and "a magical bookshop at midnight" for Zumi — 2026-10-15

Research date: 2026-10-15. Method: primary sources where they exist (official sites, press kits, developer and art-director interviews, art-book listings, Meta developer docs). Where no primary source states a palette, the palette was **measured**: the 400 free stills Studio Ghibli publishes on ghibli.jp (50 per film, "use freely within common sense") were downloaded for eight films and each still was reduced to 320×180, classed per pixel as warm (hue <70° or >330°, S>0.12), cool (180–290°) or neutral, quantised to 6 colours (median cut), and the mean of the brightest 10% of warm pixels ("lamp") and darkest third of cool pixels ("shadow") taken. Contrast ratios are WCAG 2.1 relative-luminance computations. Anything marked **[unverified]** rests on a review, fan page or search excerpt rather than a primary source; **[measured]** is from the script above, not from a published palette. Audience: daily webtoon readers 14–30, male-leaning, geek/otaku, large Asian share.

## Executive summary

1. **Lofi is a lighting model, not a colour.** Every canonical lofi scene (Lofi Girl's room, Coffee Talk's café, Spiritfarer's boat at night) is one warm key light inside a cool blue field, with the window as the cool source and a lamp or screen as the warm one. Ghibli night interiors measure the same way: warm pixels 55–93% of the frame with a blue-black shadow floor of `#0C171C`–`#2C303A`, never neutral grey **[measured]**.
2. **Ghibli's cosiness is traditional paint on paper, soft-edged and low-chroma.** Takeshige: all backgrounds except *Earwig* are "painted on paper using paint"; Oga: ~21 Nicker poster colours, "subdued but beautiful", "not piercing, raw primary colors". There is no hard bloom; lamps are painted as a value gradient on the surfaces they hit, and the brightest warm areas in night stills sit at only `#B4–#DC` red, i.e. a cream-amber, not a saturated orange **[measured]**.
3. **"Magical bookshop" games agree on five materials** (worn wood, brass, glass, leather, paper) and one trick: the UI is made of the shop's own stationery. Tiny Bookshop runs custom shaders that add "paper grain, slight wobble and a brushstroke effect to all the UI", and lets ambient light tint the UI by time of day; Strange Horticulture's guide is a book and page turns are 3D.
4. **Reading apps already own "sepia" (`#FBF0D9`/`#5F4B32`, Kindle for PC 2011) and third-party manga dimmers ship Paper/Dusk/Midnight warmth presets.** Zumi should name its themes the same way (Paper / Dusk / Midnight) so the audience reads them as reader modes, not as branding.
5. **Proposed tokens**: paper `#F5EDDC`, aged paper `#E8DCC4`, ink `#2A211B`, accent coffee `#8C5A33` (4.97:1 on paper, AA text), lamp `#E0A24A` (7.89:1 on midnight), midnight `#121A22` with raised surface `#1B2430`. Dark mode is "the shop at midnight": indigo-black ground, amber lamp accents, cream text `#EDE4D3` (13.9:1), never `#121212`.
6. **Keep the energy**: badges, rankings and live counters use the lamp colour and the orb's glow, the only things allowed to animate; everything else is still paper. That is what stops cosy from reading as sleepy to a Kakao/Bilibili audience.
7. **XR**: Quest 3 budgets (72 FPS minimum, draw-call-sensitive, baked lighting for statics) favour exactly the Ghibli model: one baked warm key, a cool ambient, and a few emissive "floating light" sprites; passthrough adds 20–40% GPU cost, so MR reading uses additive-blend glow panels, not lit geometry.

## 2b. Studio Ghibli

Primary statements. Yoji Takeshige (art director, *Spirited Away*, *The Wind Rises*, *The Boy and the Heron*): with the exception of *Earwig* "all of the background art is basically painted on paper using paint"; what shapes a scene is "the humidity of the air, along with how it spreads light"; he keeps "the atmosphere, light, and a sense of everyday life" in mind; Miyazaki's note: "Make it scenery that looks familiar" ([It's Nice That, 2026](https://www.itsnicethat.com/articles/yoji-takeshige-painting-the-worlds-of-studio-ghibli-publication-animation-050226); the 844-plate book is [PIE International](https://pie.co.jp/book/i/5954)). Kazuo Oga (*Totoro*, *Mononoke*, *Spirited Away* opening): Nicker poster colour, about 21 colours, "even with a small number of colors, you can create a wide range of colors through subtle mixing"; "if the color gradations are combined well, you can get colors that look like transparent watercolor"; Miyazaki asked for "subdued but beautiful (kirei) colors"; happy colours are "not piercing, raw primary colors, but softer in some way, colors that make you feel relieved"; "the color of a pillar also changes according to how the light hits it" ([Animation Obsessive 1](https://animationobsessive.substack.com/p/what-kazuo-oga-thinks-about-when), [2](https://animationobsessive.substack.com/p/defining-the-world-of-my-neighbor), [ANN 2008](https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/news/2008-04-30/ghibli-background-artist-kazuo-oga-interviewed)). Oga wets the paper, drops paint in and blends while wet, large soft passages first and details last ([Gurney Journey](http://gurneyjourney.blogspot.com/2017/03/demo-by-kazuo-oga.html)). *Arrietty*: Takeshige did the human world, Noboru Yoshida the world under the floor, deliberately two styles; the staff "struggled" with scale and maximised the "giant" props ([Halcyon Realms](https://halcyonrealms.com/anime/the-art-of-kari-gurashi-arrietty-book-review/)). *Earwig*: the witch's workshop took one artist a year; Goro Miyazaki explicitly avoided photorealism ([/Film](https://www.slashfilm.com/579148/goro-miyazaki-interview-earwig-and-the-witch/)). *The Cat Returns*: art director Naoya Tanaka ([ANN](https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/encyclopedia/anime.php?id=868)); his line "It's not a naturalistic effect" is in the Oga piece. Ghibli Museum's Tri Hawks reading room holds books Miyazaki recommends for children to "see, touch, and feel"; no primary description of its finishes was found ([ghibli-museum.jp](https://www.ghibli-museum.jp/en/welcome/)). Stills: ghibli.jp released 400 stills in Sept 2020 with Suzuki's line "常識の範囲でご自由にお使いください" ([ghibli.jp](https://www.ghibli.jp/info/013344/)); the `works/<film>/` pages for *mimi*, *howl*, *majo*, *totoro*, *karigurashi*, *baron*, *aya*, *chihiro* each expose 50 `gallery/*.jpg`.

What the numbers say. (1) **Ratio**: lamp-lit rooms are 55–93% warm pixels; moonlit or unlit rooms flip to 45–64% cool; the two states rarely mix in one frame except at the lamp (majo042: 41/33, the clearest "lamp in a blue room" split). (2) **The lamp is cream, not orange**: the brightest warm areas average `#A1967F`–`#DCD1AC` in hand-painted films and only go to `#D6966D` in CG *Earwig*; saturation of the lamp region stays around 25–40%. (3) **The shadow is never grey**: darkest cool pixels sit at `#07191F`–`#2C303A`, a blue-black with green or violet bias. (4) **Mean luminance 0.19–0.33**: night scenes are dark but with 10–26% of the frame in a mid cream (`#948F7B`–`#C4AB96`), which is where the faces and the paper go. (5) **Materials** read as hue shifts of one brown: wood `#503D29`→`#6A5644`, brass `#A7884F`, paper/cloth `#AB987A`→`#C4AB96`, glass as a sage/grey-green `#8D9D89` (howl008). (6) **Clutter composition**: shelves run to the frame edge and the human subject occupies a small, centred, lit pocket (mimi012, howl008, karigurashi028); scale is pushed by furniture drawn large relative to the figure, which *Arrietty* makes literal. (7) **Small lights as characters**: soot sprites (totoro003, chihiro014) and Calcifer (howl041) are the only saturated elements in their frames, which is why they read as the mascot.

Ghibli-derived token set (hex from the measurements, lifted 1–2 steps for screen legibility): `ghibli.paper #E9DCC0` (from `#C4AB96`/`#BDAF9F` lifted), `ghibli.wood #5A4330` (from `#503D29`/`#594438`), `ghibli.brass #B8955A` (from `#A7884F`/`#B89455`), `ghibli.lamp #DCC39A` (from `#CDAE95`/`#DCD1AC`), `ghibli.night #0F1A21` (from `#0C171C`/`#07191F`), `ghibli.nightRaised #1E2A36` (from `#2C303A`), `ghibli.accent #8D4E25` (Seiji's workshop varnish, mimi024), `ghibli.sage #8D9D89` (glass/mirror, optional secondary).

Three UI translations. **Hero banner**: night ground `#0F1A21` with a 60–70% warm mask (the Ghibli ratio) painted as a soft radial from one off-centre lamp (`#DCC39A` at 35% opacity, 600 px radius, feathered, no bloom), illustrated shelves running off the edges, the featured cover sitting in the lit pocket; the orb is the only saturated element, as Calcifer is in howl041. **Episode card hover**: at rest the card is `ghibli.paper` with ink text; on hover the card does not lift or scale but is "lamp-lit": a `box-shadow: 0 0 0 1px #B8955A55, 0 8px 24px -12px #DCC39A66` plus a 4% warm overlay (`#DCC39A0A`), 180 ms ease-out, like the pillar whose colour changes "according to how the light hits it". **XR reading space**: one baked warm key (colour `#DCC39A`, ~2700 K, from a lamp prop above and behind the reader's shoulder), ambient from a two-colour gradient skybox (`#0F1A21` horizon to `#1E2A36` zenith), emissive-only "floating lights" (soot-sprite/wisp sprites at `#DCC39A`, 2–6 of them, additive, no real-time light), and the comic panel itself as the brightest surface, which is the majo042 composition: a lamp, a blue room, a lit page.

| `midnight` | `#121A22` | dark-mode ground | The measured shadow floor `#0C171C`–`#1E2224` lifted one step; blue-black, green bias. `midnight.raised #1B2430` for cards (1.12:1 against ground, so separate with a 1 px `#B8955A33` brass hairline, not a shadow). Text `#EDE4D3` (13.9:1), muted text `#C9B99A` (9.1:1). |

Dark mode becomes the shop at midnight by holding the Ghibli ratio: ≥60% of the viewport is `midnight`, one warm pocket (hero lamp, orb, active row) in `lamp`, hairlines in brass, and no neutral greys anywhere. The comic itself is never tinted; only the chrome.

### B. Texture and light

Allowed: paper grain on chrome only, as an SVG `feTurbulence` or tiled PNG at 3–5% opacity on light, 6–8% on midnight (Tiny Bookshop's shader precedent); a page vignette of ≤8% ink at the corners on reading surfaces; "lamp on hover" (section 2b card spec); dust motes only in the hero and the end card, ≤12 particles, 20–40 s drift; halftone only inside the orb's bubbles and comic lettering. Avoid: grain on cover art or panels; bloom or `filter: blur()` glows (Ghibli paints value, not light); drop shadows on midnight (use brass hairlines); any gradient with a neutral grey stop; sepia-tinting the artwork (the dark-mode userscript's rule is the audience's expectation).

### C. Typography

Keep the brief: condensed geometric sans for UI headings with a CJK companion, one warm accent face for titles and the wordmark. Candidates on Google Fonts (specimen pages, not fetched): headings Barlow Condensed or Saira Condensed (Archivo Narrow if a less display cut is wanted); CJK companions Noto Sans KR / JP / SC (the condensed Noto cuts exist but are not on Google Fonts), or Zen Kaku Gothic New for JP and IBM Plex Sans KR for KR if a slightly warmer gothic is wanted; warm serif accent Fraunces (variable optical size, soft) or Lora, with CJK serif companions Shippori Mincho (JP, "beautiful even when the characters are enlarged", B1 cut has rounded corners and ink pooling) and Gowun Batang or Nanum Myeongjo (KR); hand-lettered accent only for the wordmark and orb bubbles, Caveat or Kalam if the artist's lettering is not ready. Render a mixed Latin/JP/KR specimen before committing; the search found no published pairing guidance for these combinations ([Shippori Mincho](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Shippori+Mincho), [Nanum Myeongjo](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Nanum+Myeongjo), [Barlow Condensed](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Barlow+Condensed), [Fraunces](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Fraunces)).

### D. Motion vocabulary

| Token | Duration / loop | Use | Precedent |
|---|---|---|---|
| `motion.lampHover` | 180 ms ease-out in, 240 ms out | card and row hover | Oga's pillar changing with light |
| `motion.breathe` | 4 s sine, glow opacity 0.6→1.0, scale 1.00→1.03 | orb at rest, "reading now" dot | lofi loop that nobody watches |
| `motion.pageTurn` | 320 ms, cubic-bezier(0.2, 0.8, 0.2, 1), 3D only on episode open | episode start/end | Strange Horticulture 3D page turns |
| `motion.drift` | 20–40 s linear loop, ≤12 motes | hero, end card | lofi dust |
| `motion.rain` | 8 s loop, 2 layers, opacity ≤15% | optional hero at night, XR window | Coffee Talk pitch |
| `motion.flicker` | never on UI; only inside the orb (±4% opacity, 2–3 Hz, random) | orb "thinking" | Calcifer |
| `motion.badgePop` | 160 ms spring overshoot 1.15 | UP / NEW badges, rank arrows | keeps platform energy |

All respect `prefers-reduced-motion` (loops stop, hover becomes instant).

### E. The Zumi orb

Zumi is the one saturated light in every frame, like the soot sprites and Calcifer in the measured stills, and like Lofi Girl's lamp. Rules: core `lamp.bright #F2B55C` with the reader's hue tint, halo painted as two soft concentric discs (opacity 0.35 and 0.12, radii 1.6× and 2.6×) rather than a blur; it casts "light" on the UI (a 4% warm overlay on the card it floats over) so it behaves like a lamp; expressions from the single `mood` parameter; at rest it reads (the book-spirit pose), breathes on `motion.breathe`, flickers only when loading. In XR it is the reader's avatar and the reading lamp at once.

### F. XR translation (Quest 3)

Budgets: Meta requires a minimum 72 FPS (Quest 3 also 90/120 Hz), is draw-call sensitive, and asks for fewer draw calls and simpler shaders ([Meta perf](https://developers.meta.com/horizon/documentation/unity/po-perf-opt-mobile/), [device comparison](https://developers.meta.com/horizon/resources/device-optimization-comparison/)); Unity's mobile guidance is to bake static lighting and keep dynamic lights to a minimum because real-time lights break instancing ([Unity](https://unity.com/how-to/mobile-game-optimization-tips-part-2), [Unity issue](https://issuetracker.unity3d.com/issues/xr-quest-urp-increasing-the-amount-of-real-time-lights-breaks-gpu-instancing)); passthrough costs roughly 20–40% of GPU depending on API **[unverified: Mixed News quoting Meta]** ([Mixed](https://mixed-news.com/en/meta-quest-3-vr-games-post-processing/)). Meta's passthrough guidance: match virtual lighting to the room, alpha blend for opaque objects, additive blend for a "holographic" look, use Environment Depth for occlusion, and do not put dark text on transparent panels ([Meta passthrough](https://developers.meta.com/horizon/essentials/horizon-os-passthrough/)).

Full-VR "midnight shop": one baked warm key from a lamp prop (`#DCC39A`), gradient skybox `#0F1A21`→`#1E2A36`, lightmaps for shelves, light probes for the orb and hands, 2–6 emissive wisp sprites (additive, unlit), the comic page as an unlit emissive quad at the top of the value range, a single real-time point light only on the orb if budget allows (it is the mascot; it may be the one dynamic light). MR passthrough reading: no lit geometry at all; the page and the chrome are unlit `paper`/`midnight` panels, the orb and badges use additive-blend glow so they add light to the real room (Meta's holographic mode), and the room's own lamps supply the warm key. Dark-room fallback: when passthrough is dim, switch chrome to midnight tokens and raise the orb's emissive, since passthrough quality drops in low light **[unverified: Mixed News]**.

### G. Risks

Kitsch: avoid candles, fairy-lights, cursive and gold filigree as UI elements; the magic is the orb, the lamp-hover and the night sky, nothing else. Low contrast on paper: `accent.coffee` is only 4.97:1, so caramel/lamp are fills with ink text, and muted text on paper must stay ≥ `#6B4423` (7.3:1). Cultural mismatch: the male otaku audience lives in dark, dense, badge-heavy platforms (Kakao `#121212`, Bilibili `#1C1C1F`); the mitigation is that dark mode is a real night room with the same density, rankings and `UP` badges, in `lamp` rather than green or pink, and that the motion budget is spent on exactly those live signals. Sleepiness: a lofi frame can read as "nothing is happening"; counters, rank arrows and `badgePop` keep time visible.
